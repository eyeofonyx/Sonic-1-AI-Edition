# Classic Sonic artwork, layouts and music

Sonic the Hedgehog (1991) and Sonic the Hedgehog 2 (1992) graphics, original level data and music are copyright their respective owners, including SEGA. This is an unofficial fan-made tribute, not an official SEGA product.

Primary data sources:
- Sonic Retro Sonic 1 disassembly, AS branch: https://github.com/sonicretro/s1disasm/tree/AS
- Sonic Retro Sonic 2 disassembly: https://github.com/sonicretro/s2disasm
- Music captures and VGM loop metadata: Video Game Music Preservation Foundation, https://www.vgmpf.com/Wiki/index.php/Sonic_the_Hedgehog_(GEN)

## Original nineteen-stage adventure
The adventure replaces the earlier handcrafted courses with the original Sonic 1 Green Hill, Marble, Spring Yard, Labyrinth, Star Light and Scrap Brain Acts 1–3, followed by Final Zone. Foreground and background layout bytes, 256px chunks, 16px block mappings, collision indices, signed collision heightmaps, angle maps, start positions, original ring groups and object coordinates are decoded from the AS/REV01 data. Original files with REV01 alternatives are used where applicable.

`original/ghz-chunks.png` and `original/mz-chunks.png` contain original native-resolution chunks. Backgrounds use their original layout order, rather than recombining unrelated scenery fragments. Original high-priority 8px patterns provide foreground occlusion.

`original/objects.png` contains additional native platforms, collapsing ledges, Marble grass platforms, masonry, glass pillars, crushers, green blocks, switches, swinging platforms, breakable walls, spike helixes and Newtron frames assembled from the original sprite mappings and art. Origins, tile flips and palettes are retained.

The browser game uses these original layouts and placements, but its collision solver, moving-object behavior, enemy AI and Egg Mobile encounters are browser implementations, not the original executable game or a ROM emulator. The Sonic 2 spin dash and optional Crisp jump remain explicit additions. The Green Hill and Marble eight-hit Egg Mobile finales use a wrecking ball and falling/spreading fire respectively; Spring Yard adds its eight-hit floor-breaking encounter. Labyrinth retains an upward chase rather than requiring eight hits. All five animal capsules and buttons retain their original positions. The custom Marble Sentinel is no longer part of the adventure.

## Original music
`../audio/sonic1-hill.ogg`, `sonic1-marble.ogg`, `sonic1-boss.ogg`, and `sonic1-clear.ogg` are original Sonic 1 soundtrack captures from the preservation archive above, not the app's previous improvised note sequences. Music is credited to Masato Nakamura / SEGA. Zone and boss loops use the original VGM's sample-count loop boundaries. The local files need no runtime third-party music or sprite service.

Pause, sound mute, title return, act changes and special-stage transitions share the game's audio controls.

## Original title and hidden level select
`original/title-background.png` replaces all handmade title scenery with the original GHZ background tile layout, decoded using Sonic 1's title palette. Four native water palette phases advance every six game frames; reduced motion freezes palette cycling and scrolling. The original title emblem and finger-wag sprites remain at their native proportions in a 320×224 view. The existing original `sonic1-title.ogg` now plays on the first title interaction (browsers require a gesture to enable audio); returning to the title restarts the same one-shot theme, with shared mute.

The external act, boss-practice and special-stage selectors have been removed. Enter **Up, Down, Left, Right**, then hold **A** and press **Enter/Start** on the title to open the in-game level select; the original ring cue confirms unlocking. Wrong directional sequences reset the sequence, held-key repeats do not advance it, and normal Start still starts/resumes the adventure without A. The title controller offers the same inputs on phones; its A button latches for single-pointer access. The unlock lasts for the current app session.

The menu uses Sonic 2's original blue animated SONIC/MILES background and standard font, with native pixel lettering, yellow selection, keyboard/controller navigation, all nineteen stages, six special-stage practice entries, six finales, ending playback and a music sound test. This is an adapted Sonic 2-style menu, not a claim that Sonic 2 originally contained these Sonic 1 stages. Special-stage practice returns to this menu; earned giant-ring visits still return to the adventure. Original sources and hashes are recorded in `original/classic-menus.sources.json`; artwork remains © SEGA. No runtime third-party asset requests are required.

## Spring Yard and Labyrinth expansion
`original/syz-chunks.png`, `original/lz-chunks.png`, and the extracted expansion data contain all six original layouts, backgrounds, starts, collision indices and object placements. The full zone order is Green Hill → Marble → Spring Yard → Labyrinth → Star Light → Scrap Brain → Final Zone. Scores, lives and cleared-act markers continue across the nineteen stages. Direct act selection and practice are available through the secret in-game title menu, not outside the game.

`original/expansion-objects.png` contains native SYZ platforms, floating blocks, bumpers, small/big spiked balls, Roller, Yadrin, boss-floor blocks and the original boss spike; LZ doors, flapping doors, poles, rafts, rising platforms, corks, harpoons, bubbles and countdown digits, gargoyles, conveyor wheels/platforms, water surfaces/waterfalls, Jaws, Burrobot and Orbinaut. Sprite bounds/origins are retained and the same computed spike-ball/harpoon positions drive drawing and damage. The water palettes and initial zone palette-cycle colors come from the disassembly; the additional water atlases are retained alongside the palette-based browser rendering.

Spring Yard implements pinball bounce with a cooldown, swinging spike chains, oscillating/circling large spike balls, moving block formations, switch doors, the SYZ3 transporting block, spiky-top Yadrin and rolling Roller. Its boss targets a floor block, dives, lifts it and removes it from the ten-block bridge. It takes eight hits; disappearing floor remains dangerous.

Labyrinth reduces normal acceleration/braking/friction and running cap by half (3 px/frame cap), uses a 3.5 px/frame jump, 2 px/frame jump-release limit and 16/256 gravity underwater. Entry halves horizontal speed and quarters vertical speed; exit doubles upward speed up to 16. Crisp does not replace the underwater gravity. Air is 30 seconds, refilled by a large bubble or surfacing; small bubbles do not refill it. Low-air warning cues, original countdown digits/music, the drowning sound and drowning pose accompany the countdown. Air loss ignores rings, shields and invincibility. Death and checkpoint respawns restore air; water phase, altered slide chunk and switches are restored from the checkpoint.

Original dynamic-water thresholds, the initial LZ3 flood/slide switch, current rectangles and slide chunk speeds are used. Switches open the appropriate doors; flapping doors alternate collision with their open/closed state; poles break under sustained contact; corks respond to the water. Conveyor corner coordinates and platform spawn records come directly from Object 63 and the original platform position files. Moving platforms carry Sonic. Harpoons, spike chains, gargoyle projectiles and the zone's native badniks fill the original placements. The final Labyrinth shaft uses the original terrain/trap positions and rising water, with an Egg Mobile chase that can be completed by reaching the upper exit without eight hits, then opening the capsule. This is still browser-authored behavior, not a frame-identical port or emulator; timings, AI, palette effects and collision response may differ.

Both SYZ2 exit routes retain their separate original giant rings. The ≥50-ring threshold, six-emerald restriction and original act tally apply in the new zones too.

The locally bundled `sonic1-spring.ogg`, `sonic1-labyrinth.ogg` and `sonic1-drowning.ogg` are VGMPF captures. Original VGM timings are Spring Yard 2.299183673–40.699183673 seconds, Labyrinth 2.628321995–37.194988662 seconds, and the one-shot drowning countdown 11.562222222 seconds. Added bumper, splash, get-air, air-warning and drowning recordings come from Sonic Worlds Next, with URLs/hashes in the existing sound manifest. Pause/mute and state-preserving special-stage visits apply to these as well.

## Opening, native HUD and title cards
The app opens with the original REV01 SEGA logo, decoded from its Nemesis tile art, two Enigma tile planes and final Sega2 palette. The palette scan is adapted to a brief fade, followed by the original mapped SONIC TEAM PRESENTS credit lettering and the existing title. The local SEGA PCM chant comes directly from the disassembly. Tap the logo to hear it; browsers require interaction for audio. Start/Escape or Skip intro advances to the title, enabling its original theme after a gesture. Returning to the title does not unnecessarily repeat boot. Reduced motion removes fades; skip and mute remain accessible.

The adventure's replacement HUD uses the original HUD Nemesis art, HUD Numbers, Lives Counter Numbers and Sonic life icon: native SCORE/E, TIME/colon, RING/S, 8×16 counters and 8×8 life digits. Values are right-aligned without artificial leading zeros; zero rings flash using the native eight-frame bit. The added boss-health banner was removed from the original playfield. Native mapped zone-name, ZONE, ACT and blue-oval title cards replace the generic rectangle; Final Zone correctly has no act numeral. Cards animate with the gameplay clock and pause with it.

## Star Light, Scrap Brain and Final Zone
The seven added stages use original AS/REV01 layouts, starts, backgrounds, chunks, collisions, ring groups and placements. `original/slz-chunks.png`, `sbz-chunks.png`, `sbz2-chunks.png`, `sbz3-chunks.png` and `finale-objects.png` are locally bundled native assets. Scrap Brain Act 3 uses its own Labyrinth-based geometry and surface/underwater palettes, not a third industrial act invented for the browser. Final Zone uses the original SBZ2 terrain bank with its separate FZ start and object list, with no rings.

Star Light adds fans, native stair blocks, orbiting platforms, elevators, seesaws and spike balls, walking bombs and Orbinauts. Its eight-hit seesaw boss drops spike balls onto the original seesaws, which launch Sonic or return a ball into Eggman. It retains its original capsule/button placement. Scrap Brain adds its native saws, flamethrowers, electrical hazards, timed spin/vanishing platforms, crushers, sliding switch platforms, girders, conveyors, bombs and Ball Hogs. Platform-conveyor corner paths and child spawn records are decoded from Object 6F and all six native spawn files. SBZ2's tubes use Object 72's eight original target routes, including its 50-ring gate and signed/vertically wrapping coordinates. Its final Eggman/floor-trap scene leads directly into Act 3 without a counterfeit eight-hit boss or tally. SBZ3 has water/air/bubbles and its switch-operated ancient lift; its high exit leads directly into Final Zone.

Final Zone alternates moving cylinder pairs and four descending plasma balls with a dodge gap. Its lower cylinders have physical cap platforms, and contact with closing machinery is lethal. Sonic can strike the exposed side of Eggman's occupied lower cylinder while rolling/jumping; eight hits trigger explosions and escape, then the ending, without a capsule or act tally. Final practice correctly starts with zero rings; other boss practices keep twelve. Machinery, AI, attack selection, collision response and timing are browser-authored recreations; this is not executable original game code or a frame-perfect emulator.

## Super Peel Out
A Sonic CD-inspired standing dash is available throughout the adventure. On flat or gentle ground, hold Up plus Space/W/Z for 30 fixed game frames (half a second), then release Up to launch at 12 px/frame, or 6 underwater. Sonic stays standing and vulnerable to badniks rather than receiving the spin dash's rolling attack. Early release, releasing Jump while still holding Up, pressing Down, hurt/death, loss of ground, currents/slides, springs, pause, restart and scene transitions cancel charging. Up now looks up instead of jumping in the adventure; Space/W/Z remain jump controls. Special-stage Up-jump controls are unchanged.

The charge now accelerates from the native Sonic 1 walk/run pictures into Sonic CD's original figure-eight peel-out animation. The sixteen CD mapping frames **0x94–0xA3** provide four frames for each of four slope/loop angle banks; sprite flips follow the existing octant logic. Their native mapping origins, pixel proportions and Sonic palette are preserved, without rotating or stretching the artwork. Running at 10 px/frame or faster retains the CD cycle, reverting to the ordinary run/walk animation as speed drops; rolling, jumping, hurt and death retain their existing priorities. Charge duration, launch speeds, underwater rules and controls are unchanged. The CD art, palette, mapping, DPLC and animation data were decoded from `sonicretro/scddisasm`, pinned to commit `f8cf3f0af7bf419d18182ba98554f09307ad3cc6`. The bundled `original/cd-peel-out.sources.json` records exact source locations and SHA-256 hashes. Original Sonic CD artwork © SEGA; this remains a browser recreation rather than an emulator.

The two peel-out sound recordings are locally bundled from GVB Sonic (`PeelCharge.wav` and `PeelRelease.wav`), with source URLs and hashes in the sound manifest. They share mute and cancelable playback with the other effects. Keyboard, hold/release phone controls, a two-tap accessible charge/release button, an in-game readiness label and a charge meter expose the same mechanic. Existing Sonic 2 spin dash remains separate.

## Water, loop and finale quality-of-life fixes
Labyrinth now draws native above-water and underwater atlases in two clipped passes at the waterline. The submerged terrain palettes are read as complete 128-byte / 64-color files. Unlike the 96-byte dry zone files, they already include Sonic's first color line; prepending that line again shifts the stonework onto the salmon badnik colors. Labyrinth and Scrap Brain 3 now both retain the correct four-line ordering. Terrain, Sonic, CD Peel Out, rings, badniks, shields, platforms and switches retain source palette indices until rasterization; shared dry RGB values no longer collapse different CRAM entries into one underwater color. Opaque black artwork no longer gets confused with the underwater backdrop. Scrap Brain 3 has its own corresponding palettes, including the ancient lift. The HUD remains legible and separate from the water pass. `original/water-palettes.sources.json` identifies the source palettes and parent extraction manifests. No runtime RGB color-key conversion or third-party requests are used.

Labyrinth Act 3's lower water slide now wraps across the original 2048px vertical seam instead of falling out of the level. Momentum and the camera transfer to the returning route; the wrap is limited to the slide and its short exit grace period, not a general pit-rescue or invulnerability feature. Native air, bubbles and hazards remain active. Jump presses cannot accidentally detach Sonic while the slide is carrying him. Star Light uses its original $AA/$B4 loop-plane rules, including airborne behavior for the second type and alternate collision chunks, alongside the existing GHZ rules. Actual supported traversal through the apex and back out has been checked, not just angle-state toggles.

The Star Light boss now aims at the original three boss seesaws and drops full 24×24 mapped spike balls on an elapsed-fight-time schedule. The old renderer incorrectly used out-of-range 8×8 explosion-fragment mappings. Fresh drops cannot hurt their owner; only returned balls score a hit. A ball landing on one end raises the opposite end and launches Sonic if he is there; landing on the opposite end of a loaded seesaw sends the ball back up. Native slope/flat profiles and artwork share their tilt, with platform-rider correction. The boss's pipe is visible, and leftover boss spike balls are cleared on defeat.

Final Zone uses one geometry definition for its native cylinder origins, cap edges, solid bodies and 160px displacement. The ceiling origins are corrected by 48px. Cylinders block from the side, support Sonic on lower caps, and stop upward jumps at their undersides; collision with a body is not an automatic proximity death. Closing machinery only crushes when Sonic runs out of clearance against a solid floor/ceiling or is trapped between adjacent cylinders. An Eggman hit no longer grants immunity to all machinery for the rest of the cycle. Movement, drawn cap positions and collision surfaces advance on the same fixed step. A normal jump from the unobstructed side can land a hit and rebound safely. Plasma and eight-hit progression remain active.

The ending waits for its artwork before advancing, has a continuous run-in with distance-based native leg cadence, braking and a turn, and uses the original hold/confused picture sequences with a final-frame hold. The native leap pictures are given a clearer transition, the logo eases into place, and emeralds orbit at a stable rate rather than accelerating erratically. Animals arrive from both sides using their native walking/flapping pairs and no longer all disappear at the leap. Pause and page visibility freeze the choreography and music together; reduced-motion alternatives, both final poses, replay and credits remain available. Space activates focused ending buttons instead of being intercepted as a pause command.

## Adventure reliability polish
All 25 placed checkpoints activate by their original checkpoint ID and actual contact, rather than assuming that progression always moves right. This fixes the backward route in Scrap Brain 3 and avoids activating distant posts. Checkpoints restore their saved elapsed time, water, switches, layout, platform positions/travel and mechanism phase; opened doors do not close again for their first few respawn frames. Entering a boss fight captures an arena-height checkpoint, replacing any earlier route's altitude; a death cannot use an unrelated old checkpoint's Y position. Respawn cameras are bounded to the stage. Restarting an ordinary act restores the score and lives carried into that act, resets its pickups, and retains that entry bank across checkpoint deaths; boss practice still restarts fresh.

Score awards from rings, monitors, enemies, checkpoint posts, breakables and bosses share the 50,000-point extra-life logic. The 100/200-ring lives are awarded once per life/act, so losing and recollecting the same 100 rings cannot repeatedly grant lives. Underwater hurt recoil is halved, matching underwater movement. Input while paused, dying or clearing cannot queue a jump or charge for later; uppercase movement/jump keys are normalized. Phone controls visibly disable while paused, and the dedicated Peel button cannot accidentally jump when charging is unavailable. Backgrounding suspends gameplay and preview audio, and resuming wakes the audio context. Help and sound-test dialogs trap focus, close consistently with Escape, restore focus, and only resume an adventure that was running when opened. A hidden tab cannot silently resume a sound preview.

Special-stage practice retains its original emerald ceremony but cannot award adventure emeralds or its ending variation. Starting a fresh adventure after Final Zone clears the previous run's emerald collection and re-enables giant rings. Ending/maze resume controls wake interrupted audio; credits can be replayed directly and starting them from a paused ending unpauses their presentation.

LZ block frame 3 uses a packed negative tile offset: the mapping word must be added to the original VRAM base before reading palette/flip bits. Retaining those carries restores its native terrain palette and orientation instead of the incorrectly pink, mirrored block. This is applied to the dry/underwater LZ and SBZ3 object atlases without altering the corrected terrain palettes or collision shape.

Verification covers all nineteen stage starts/pause states, all 25 checkpoints, all six boss retry altitudes, ordinary act handoffs, restart banks, reward thresholds, input/audio suspension and the original LZ block pixels. Existing loop, slide, water, finale, special-stage, CD Peel Out and audio checks remain relevant. This is targeted verification, not a claim of a complete nineteen-stage manual playthrough or a frame-perfect port.

## Drop Dash and Super Sonic

Drop Dash is a browser adaptation of Mania's second-jump-button hold: release the initial jump, press/hold Jump again for 20 fixed steps, and keep holding through contact. It uses Mania's directional landing formula (8 base / 12 cap, or 12 / 13 as Super); underwater launch is halved. A released, paused, hurt, spring-launched or externally controlled charge cannot produce a delayed landing dash. Existing Sonic 1 roll pictures accelerate while ready; no additional image rotation is applied. Native source rules are pinned to RSDKModding/Sonic-Mania-Decompilation `9dc699428420d752af9767bdb13f585ee0881bc0`, `Player_State_DropDash` and `Player_JumpAbility_Sonic`.

Super Sonic requires six **earned** Sonic 1 emeralds, or playing the numbered Sound Test sequence **04, 01, 02, 06**. Both the blue menu and the accessible sound room accept the sequence. Choosing a number alone does not count; the play buttons enter numbers even when muted. A wrong number resets the sequence. The code unlocks transformation for this app session, not adventure emeralds, giant-ring completion, or the earned ending. Native Sonic 2 uses seven emeralds; retaining this game's six is deliberate.

With 50+ rings, jump and press **X** or the phone **SUPER** button. A 40-tick transformation leads to 10 px/frame running, 48/256 acceleration, 1.0 braking, 96/256 air acceleration and an 8-pixel dry jump. Existing underwater reduction and 3.5 water jump remain. Super spin dash releases at 12–16 (halved underwater); Super Peel Out runs at 16/8. Super Drop Dash retains Mania's separate 12/13 parameters. Rings drain exactly once per 60 playing ticks after the transformation; pauses and background suspension freeze the drain. Zero rings, death, restart, act changes, signposts/tallies and the finale end the form. Ordinary enemies, projectiles, spikes and lava cannot hurt Super Sonic; drowning, time-out, pits and physical crushing still can. Shields and timed monitor effects remain separate.

The gold form uses 111 native Sonic 2 DPLC/mapping frames, including raised-quill standing, eight-step walking, angled flight-style runs, rolling, spin dash and transformation. Source is sonicretro/s2disasm `4ef457c18fc4902396c43ab06fabc119ac6ecb64`; see `original/super-sonic.sources.json`. One compact **palette-index texture** feeds a bounded 256-small-sprite cache instead of keeping 45 large colored atlases decoded on phones. Original palette cycles affect only Sonic, never terrain or other sprites. Underwater gold uses the native ARZ transformation colors with the correct LZ/SBZ3 clothes/outline row; reduced motion holds the steady gold palette. Original Sonic 2 music uses its native VGM loop 3.455804989–23.122471655 seconds. `../audio/abilities.sources.json` pins music, Drop Dash sample and rules; transformation/release cues reuse the bundled warp and release recordings. This is an adapted browser moveset, not a frame-perfect Mania/Sonic 2 port.

## Secret Emerald Hill bonus

Play the numbered Sound Test sequence **01, 09, 09, 02, 01, 01, 02, 04** to unlock the two-act Sonic 2 Emerald Hill bonus for this session. Both Sound Tests accept it; merely selecting numbers does not enter them. Played numbers work while muted. A prefix-aware matcher resets cleanly after wrong numbers. This code is independent of 04–01–02–06: it neither grants Super Sonic nor awards any adventure emeralds. The entry appears in the sound room, below the secret blue menu, and beside the main adventure's audio options once unlocked.

Emerald Hill runs in a separate engine instance with its own score/lives/rings, checkpoint snapshots, time, enemies, machinery, and act bank. The Sonic 1 instance is suspended, not restarted or converted. Returning restores its previous playing/paused/title-menu state; bonus gains, losses and completion cannot change the nineteen original stages, emerald collection or earned ending. Both acts can be selected after unlocking; finishing the first carries only the bonus score/lives into the second. Finishing the second offers bonus replay or return. Existing jump feel, ring sound, shared mute and earned/code-unlocked Super availability carry into the bonus without merging rewards. The existing Sonic-only moves, Sonic 1 player/HUD/tally art and an adapted title card are deliberately retained; no Tails, half-pipes, other Sonic 2 zones, or original Sonic 2 executable are added.

Native layouts, rings, source objects, artwork and collision are pinned to sonicretro/s2disasm `4ef457c18fc4902396c43ab06fabc119ac6ecb64`. `original/emerald-hill.sources.json` records verified source hashes. The original 128px chunks remain in one 2048² atlas; virtual 256px quads adapt only the collision interface.
